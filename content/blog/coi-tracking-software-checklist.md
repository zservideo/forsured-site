Most COI tracking software demos look alike, and most lists of COI tracking software features read alike too. Someone uploads a clean certificate, the fields fill in, a green check appears, and an expiration reminder gets scheduled. The differences that matter on a construction project don't show up until a contractor's endorsement is wrong, a project carries its own limits, or a policy lapses halfway through the job.

This COI tracking software checklist is for general contractors' risk, compliance and project teams writing down what they need before they shortlist tools. It names no vendors. It lists 12 points, ranks the COI tracking software features by how much they matter, and gives a test you can run with your own documents.

The order is the weighting. Points 1 to 5 decide whether a gap gets caught at all, so they count most. Points 6 to 9 decide how fast it gets fixed and who's accountable. Points 10 to 12 decide whether the tool fits your systems, your security review and your budget.

## What should a general contractor look for in COI tracking software?

A general contractor should look for COI tracking software that holds requirements by project, reads endorsements instead of checkboxes, sends gaps back to the broker, tracks renewals across every project a contractor works on, and makes clear who approves each contractor COI. COI tracking software features like integrations, security and price come after those, because a tool that misses a gap is expensive at any price.

## What is on the COI tracking software checklist?

The COI tracking software checklist below has 12 points. The test column is what to bring or ask in the demo.

Table: COI tracking software checklist: 12 points and how to test each one
| # | Look for | How to test it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Requirements held by project and by trade | Load two projects with different umbrella limits and check one contractor against both |
| 2 | Where each requirement came from | Ask to see the contract section a requirement points back to |
| 3 | Endorsement review, not checkbox reading | Upload a certificate with the additional insured box checked and no endorsement |
| 4 | Ongoing and completed operations checked separately | Send an ongoing operations form with no completed operations form |
| 5 | Each failure explained, missing documents treated as fails | Leave out the waiver of subrogation and see how the result reads |
| 6 | The broker in the fix | Ask who gets the deficiency request and whether it quotes the requirement |
| 7 | Renewals and lapses tracked by policy | Ask what happens on the expiration date if no renewal has come in |
| 8 | Wrap-ups and blanket policies | Ask how an OCIP or CCIP enrollment changes what a contractor must show |
| 9 | Who makes the final call | Ask what's automated, what a person reviews and how accuracy is tested |
| 10 | Fit with your project tools | Ask what syncs with your project and accounting systems, in which direction |
| 11 | Security and document access | Ask for the SOC report and who can see each document |
| 12 | Price in writing | Ask for year-one and year-two totals at your contractor count |

## Which COI tracking software features matter most?

The COI tracking software features that matter most are the first five on the list, because they decide whether a gap is caught before a trade is on site. Here's what to look for in each group.

### Project requirements (points 1 and 2)

Project requirements change from job to job. One owner wants $10 million in umbrella limits, another wants $5 million, and a public project adds its own forms. A tool that keeps one set of requirements per contractor will pass a certificate that fails the project it's for. Look for project requirements set by project and by trade tier, with each line traced back to the contract section it came from. Our [state COI requirements pages](/coi-requirements/) show how much the rules vary before an owner adds anything.

### Endorsement review (points 3 to 5)

Endorsement review is where most gaps hide. An [additional insured](https://www.irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/additional-insured) is someone not automatically insured under a policy, so the endorsement, not the checkbox, is what extends coverage to you. Good endorsement review reads the form itself, checks ongoing operations and completed operations as separate items, and treats primary and noncontributory, waiver of subrogation and per project aggregate as their own lines. When something fails, the result should say what's missing and what the project needs. Our guide to [endorsement review in COI tracking software](/blog/coi-tracking-software-endorsement-review/) goes deeper on what to expect.

### Fixing gaps and keeping every contractor COI current (points 6 to 8)

A gap is only half found until the contractor's broker fixes it. Look for a tool that sends the deficiency request to the broker with the requirement quoted, takes the corrected document back and re-checks it, and keeps an audit trail of each round. Then look at time. A contractor COI that passed in March can lapse in July, so the tool should track each policy across every project that contractor works on, ask for renewals with endorsements before expiration, and treat no renewal on the expiration date as a lapse. If you run wrap-ups, ask how an OCIP or CCIP changes what each enrolled contractor must show.

### Who decides, fit, security and price (points 9 to 12)

Two COI tracking software features are easy to skip in a demo: who decides, and how the tool fits your systems. Ask the vendor to say plainly what's automated and what a person reviews. The [NAIC model bulletin on AI systems](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/inline-files/2023-12-4%20Model%20Bulletin_Adopted_0.pdf) asks insurers to govern how they use AI, and the same questions fit a COI tool: how it's tested, how errors are caught and who signs off on an approval or a waiver. For fit, check what syncs with your project tools, and look for a current listing in the [Procore Marketplace](https://marketplace.procore.com/) if you run Procore. For security, ask for the vendor's [SOC report](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/resources/landing/system-and-organization-controls-soc-suite-of-services) and use our [vendor security questions](/blog/coi-tracking-software-security-questions/). For price, get it in writing at your contractor count, and compare it with [what COI tracking software costs](/blog/coi-tracking-software-cost/) on published price lists.

## How do you use the checklist in a demo?

Use the COI tracking software checklist as a score sheet. Mark each of the 12 points pass, partial or fail for every vendor, weight points 1 to 5 most heavily, and run the tests with your own documents rather than the vendor's sample.

1. Pick one active project and pull its insurance requirements.
2. Bring three documents: a certificate with the additional insured box checked and no endorsement, a contractor COI with an umbrella below the project limit, and a renewal that's due soon.
3. Ask the vendor to load the project requirements and review the documents live.
4. Watch what happens to each failure: how it's explained, who gets the fix request, and when the item clears.
5. Ask the questions in points 9 to 12 and get the answers in writing.
