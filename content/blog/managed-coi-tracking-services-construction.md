There are three ways to manage contractor COIs: a spreadsheet, software your team runs, or a service that runs it for you. This guide is about the third. It compares the managed options from the tools in our [full comparison of COI tracking software for construction](/blog/best-coi-tracking-software-construction/), using each vendor's own pages, linked under Sources.

If you're still deciding between the three, start with our COI Journal report on [spreadsheet, service or software](https://coijournal.com/reports/spreadsheet-service-software/?utm_source=forsured).

## What a service does and doesn't take off your plate

**It takes:** reading certificates and endorsements, sending deficiency notices, chasing vendors and brokers, and renewal follow-up.

**It doesn't take:** the decision to let a trade on site with a gap, the requirements you set, or the loss if the review was wrong. Your contract with the service decides how much of that they stand behind, so read it.

## Questions to ask any managed COI service

1. Who are the reviewers, and what credential do they hold? Billy names CRIS certification; ask the others.
2. Do they read endorsement wording, or check that a form number is present?
3. How do they record an accepted gap, and can you see it?
4. What happens when you disagree with a result?
5. How many attempts do they make before escalating to you? bcs says there's no cap.
6. What's the minimum? bcs publishes $10,000 a year. Ask the rest.
7. Do your subcontractors or their brokers ever pay or sign up?

## When software beats a service

- Your owners' requirements vary by project and you want your own risk team holding the line.
- You want the broker working directly from the gap, not from a service's email.
- You want one person accountable for every waiver.

That's the case Forsured is built for: a person on your team approves or waives every result, and the broker fixes the gap in the same workspace. If you want the review done for you, a managed service is the better fit.
