Contractor backlog fell sharply in October. Associated Builders and Contractors reported Nov. 10 that its Construction Backlog Indicator dropped to 8.1 months, down 0.6 months from September and 0.3 months from October 2025. For owners and GCs, a thinner pipeline means more contractors chasing each job, and that's worth a closer look at the COIs that come with the bids.

## What ABC reported

"Backlog fell sharply in October, returning to levels not consistently observed since last winter," said ABC Chief Economist Anirban Basu.

The split behind the number is stark:

| Group | September | October |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All contractors | 8.7 months | 8.1 months |
| Contractors without data center work (88%) | 8.3 months | 7.6 months |
| Contractors with data center work | 11.3 months | 11.5 months |

ABC notes that most contractors doing data center work have more than $100 million in annual revenue. By category, infrastructure backlog fell to 8.7 months, the lowest since May 2025, while heavy industrial backlog rose to a 15-month high.

The Construction Confidence Index was mixed. Profit margin expectations improved, while sales and staffing expectations fell. All three readings stayed above 50, the level ABC says signals expected growth over the next six months. Even so, Basu noted that "roughly 1 in 4 contractors still expect their margins to shrink over the next six months," citing recent increases in materials prices and borrowing costs.

## Why it matters for contractor COIs

When backlog shrinks, contractors widen their search for work. That can mean bidding on projects larger than their usual jobs, in a new sector, or in a new state.

Their insurance was usually bought for the work they normally do. A program that fit a $10 million job can fall short on a $50 million one:

- **Limits.** Umbrella and excess limits may sit below what a larger project requires.
- **Aggregates.** A general aggregate shared across more jobs runs thinner. A [per project aggregate](https://forsured.ai/blog/per-project-aggregate-coi/) keeps yours separate.
- **Endorsements.** Additional insured, [completed operations](https://forsured.ai/blog/completed-operations-additional-insured/) and waiver wording may not match the new project's contract.

None of that shows up in a backlog number. It shows up when you read each contractor COI against the project requirements for the job in front of you, not the contractor's last one.
