A checked additional insured box on a contractor COI usually means CG 20 10, and CG 20 10 stops covering you the day the work is done. The claims a GC or owner worries about most often show up later: a roof that leaks after turnover, a stair that fails a year in. Covering those takes completed operations coverage, and that takes its own endorsement.

## Why does CG 20 10 stop covering you at completion?

CG 20 10 stops at completion because ISO rewrote it in 1993. The original 1985 edition, CG 20 10 11 85, covered liability arising out of the contractor's "your work," which included both ongoing and completed operations. [Construction Executive](https://constructionexec.com/article/the-right-endorsement-additional-insureds/) explains that the October 1993 edition changed the wording to liability arising out of "your ongoing operations," which effectively removed additional insured status for completed operations.

::video F7jjPH1311o

[Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/magazines/mag-features/2015/06/15/371046.htm) puts it in one line: "'Ongoing operations' does not include completed operations." Every edition of CG 20 10 since 1993 works the same way.

## What counts as completed operations?

Completed operations is the contractor's work once it's finished. [IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/completed-operations) defines it as work that has been completed as the contract calls for, work finished at one site of a multi-site contract, or work that has been put to its intended use.

The trigger is when the injury or damage happens, not when the work was done. The [County of Sonoma's insurance guide](https://sonomacounty.gov/administrative-support-and-fiscal-services/human-resources/divisions-and-units/liability-and-insurance/insurance/help-and-support/contract-insurance-requirements-reference-guide/additional-insured-endorsements) makes the same point: CG 20 10 doesn't cover injury or damage after the work is done, which is a completed operations hazard that needs a separate endorsement.

## What does CG 20 37 add?

CG 20 37 adds additional insured status for completed operations, and nothing else. ISO released it in October 2001, and Construction Executive describes its sole purpose as bringing back the ability to add an additional insured to completed operations coverage. Insurance Journal notes it doesn't cover ongoing operations.

So the two forms split the job in time, and the editions have narrowed along the way:

Table: CG 20 10 and CG 20 37 editions and what each covers
| Edition | Covers you for | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| CG 20 10 11 85 | Ongoing and completed operations | Original "your work" wording |
| CG 20 10 10 93 | Ongoing operations only | "Your ongoing operations" replaced "your work" |
| CG 20 37 10 01 | Completed operations only | New form to restore completed operations |
| CG 20 10 and CG 20 37 07 04 | Same split | "Caused, in whole or in part, by" replaced "arising out of" |
| CG 20 10 and CG 20 37 04 13 | Same split | Limited to what the law permits, what the contract requires, and the lower of the contract limit or policy limit |

The 2004 change narrowed coverage to liability the contractor's acts or omissions caused, in whole or in part. The 2013 change ties coverage to your contract, so a vague insurance clause now gets you only what it clearly asks for.

## Does a blanket endorsement cover completed operations?

A blanket additional insured endorsement covers completed operations only if its wording says so. Forms like CG 20 38 and CG 20 33 add additional insureds automatically when a written contract requires it, which saves scheduling every owner and GC by name. But whether completed operations is included depends on the form and its edition. See [per written contract additional insured](/blog/per-written-contract-additional-insured/) for how those forms differ.

Watch for insurer-specific forms too. Sonoma County won't accept a proprietary completed operations endorsement that limits coverage to the current policy period, because a claim about finished work can arrive after that policy has renewed.

## How do you check completed operations on a contractor COI?

You check completed operations on a contractor COI by looking past the certificate to the endorsements and the limits:

1. Confirm your project requirements call for additional insured status for both ongoing and completed operations, and say for how long after completion.
2. Ask for both endorsements behind the checked ADDL INSD box, or a blanket form that names completed operations. Note each form number and edition.
3. Check the "products - comp/op agg" limit on the [ACORD 25](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/12/acord-25-2025-12-liability.pdf). That's the aggregate completed operations claims draw from, and it should meet your requirement.
4. Check that additional insured coverage is primary where your contract requires it, with [primary and noncontributory](/blog/primary-and-noncontributory/) wording, and that a [waiver of subrogation](/blog/waiver-of-subrogation-coi/) is attached.
5. Keep collecting certificates and endorsements after turnover for as long as completed operations coverage must last.

Forsured reads the endorsement forms attached to each contractor COI and flags an additional insured endorsement that covers ongoing operations but not completed operations, against the requirements set for that project.

## What do public owners require for completed operations?

Public owners often spell out completed operations by form number, which makes their requirements a useful model for your own. The County of Sonoma, for example, requires both CG 20 10 04 13 and CG 20 37 04 13 for work performed for the County. So a contractor working for the County needs additional insured status for ongoing operations and for completed operations, not one or the other.

The County also says it isn't limited to ISO forms and will accept equivalent language. That's a practical standard for a GC too. What matters is what the endorsement covers, not whose name is printed at the bottom of it. If a contractor COI points to an insurer's own form, read it the same way you'd read CG 20 37: who it covers, for which operations, and for how long after completion.

If you write project requirements for a public job, start from the owner's insurance section and copy its form numbers and editions into your contractor requirements word for word. Your contractors' coverage then matches what the owner expects from you.

## What else affects completed operations claims?

Two other details decide how much a completed operations claim can pay. The first is the aggregate: a general aggregate shared across every job the contractor works can be spent before your claim arrives, so ask for a [per project aggregate](/blog/per-project-aggregate-coi/). The second is state law. Anti-indemnity statutes in some states limit what additional insured requirements can ask for, so check the [contractor COI requirements for your state](/coi-requirements/).
