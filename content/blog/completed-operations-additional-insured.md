Not with the standard form alone. The ISO endorsement most contractors use to add you as an additional insured, CG 20 10, has covered only the contractor's ongoing operations since 1993. Once the work is finished, that endorsement stops responding. Coverage for finished work takes a second endorsement, CG 20 37.

## Why the standard form stops at completion

The original 1985 edition, CG 20 10 11 85, covered liability arising out of the contractor's "your work," and that included both ongoing and completed operations. Construction Executive explains that ISO's October 1993 edition changed the wording to "liability arising out of your ongoing operations," which "effectively eliminated additional insured status for completed operations."

Insurance Journal put it plainly: "'Ongoing operations' does not include completed operations."

IRMI defines completed operations as work that "has been completed as called for in a contract," work finished at one jobsite of a multi-site contract, or work "that has been put to its intended use." In other words, a lot of the claims a GC or owner worries about. A roof leaks after turnover, or someone is hurt by a finished stair. Those arrive after the CG 20 10 stops covering you.

## Where CG 20 37 comes in

ISO released CG 20 37 in October 2001. Construction Executive says the form's "sole purpose" was "to bring back the ability to add an additional insured to the completed operations coverage." Insurance Journal adds that it "does not provide coverage for ongoing operations."

So the two forms split the job in time:

| Endorsement | Covers you for | Doesn't cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CG 20 10 (since 1993) | The contractor's ongoing operations | Completed operations |
| CG 20 37 (since 2001) | The contractor's completed operations | Ongoing operations |

The April 2013 editions of both forms added limits of their own. Coverage applies only "to the extent permitted by law," won't be "broader than that which you are required by the contract or agreement to provide," and is capped at the lesser of the contract's required amount or the policy limits.

## What to check on a contractor COI

1. Confirm your project requirements call for additional insured status for both ongoing and completed operations, and for how long after completion.
2. Ask for both endorsements behind the checked AI box. Note each form number and edition.
3. Check that the edition's limits fit your contract, especially the April 2013 language tying coverage to what the contract requires.
4. Keep collecting certificates after turnover for as long as completed operations coverage must last.
