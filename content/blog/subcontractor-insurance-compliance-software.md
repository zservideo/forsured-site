"COI tracking" and "subcontractor insurance compliance" get used as if they mean the same thing. They overlap. COI tracking is collecting certificates and watching dates. Subcontractor insurance compliance is proving that each subcontractor's actual coverage meets what the contract and the owner require, for as long as it has to, and acting when it doesn't.

This guide compares the eight tools in our [full comparison of COI tracking software for construction](/blog/best-coi-tracking-software-construction/) on that wider job. Every fact comes from each vendor's own pages, linked under Sources.

## Pick on the gap you can't close

**If endorsements are the gap,** pick on review depth. PINS Advantage, TrustLayer, illumend and Forsured read wording; Billy and bcs check which forms are present. See [which COI tracking software checks endorsements](/blog/coi-tracking-software-endorsement-review/).

**If you're paying non-compliant subcontractors,** pick the tool that reaches accounting. [Billy](https://billyforinsurance.com/) lists Procore, CMiC, Sage 300, Sage Intacct, JD Edwards, Viewpoint Vista, Trimble ProjectSight and Acumatica among its integrations.

**If the certificate hides exclusions,** pick policy-level review: Jones's Policy Review add-on or bcs full service.

**If mid-term cancellations worry you,** Certificial's model, with certificates from the agent of record, is built to flag changes as they happen. It depends on the agency being on their network.

**If requirements change by owner and project, and the fix keeps dying in email,** that's what Forsured is built for: each project's requirements and exceptions, and a shared workspace where the broker sees the gap, the requirement and the project.

## Where insurance compliance stops

Prequalification, safety records and financial checks are part of subcontractor risk too. Billy covers prequalification alongside insurance; most other tools here stay on insurance. If you already run a prequalification platform, ask each COI vendor what it connects to.
