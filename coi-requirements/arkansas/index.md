# Arkansas contractor insurance requirements

> What Arkansas law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 3 employees; building work 2
- License insurance minimum: No statewide minimum
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: Sole negligence

## Workers' comp in Arkansas

Most Arkansas employers with three or more employees must carry workers' comp, and employers with two or more employees doing building or building repair work must too. A contractor that hands part of its contract to another contractor needs coverage with even one employee, and so does the contractor doing that part of the work.

Agency: [Arkansas Workers' Compensation Commission](https://labor.arkansas.gov/workers-comp/awcc-about-us/basic-facts/)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

We found no statewide general liability minimum tied to a general contractor license. Cities and counties can set their own licensing and insurance rules, so check where the project is.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Arkansas's anti-indemnity statute (Ark. Code Ann. §§ 4-56-104, 22-9-214) voids construction contract clauses that make a contractor indemnify another party for that party's sole negligence. Clauses that cover shared fault are generally allowed.

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Arkansas project

1. **Workers' comp.** A contractor below Arkansas's threshold can legally go without it, but your project requirements can still require it. Decide what you'll accept, and get an exemption or a policy in writing. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Arkansas is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** Arkansas doesn't set a statewide floor for a general contractor license, so your project requirements set the bar. Compare each limit on the COI with what the contract asks for.
3. **Additional insured.** The endorsement is a separate promise from the indemnity clause, so check both. Ask for the endorsement itself, not just a box ticked on the certificate.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
