# Alabama contractor insurance requirements

> What Alabama law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

Reviewed Oct 28, 2026 by Forsured. Source: https://forsured.ai/coi-requirements/alabama/

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 5 employees; new homes 1
- License insurance minimum: No statewide minimum
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: No general statute

## Workers' comp in Alabama

Employers that regularly employ five or more people, counting corporate officers, must carry workers' comp. The five-employee floor does not apply to building new single-family detached homes: employers doing that work need coverage at any headcount.

Agency: [Alabama Department of Labor, Workers' Compensation Division](https://labor.alabama.gov/wc/insurance.aspx)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

We found no statewide general liability minimum tied to a general contractor license. Cities and counties can set their own licensing and insurance rules, so check where the project is.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Alabama has no general construction anti-indemnity statute. Courts there generally enforce a clause that covers a party's own negligence only when the clause says so clearly (see Craig Constr. Co. v. Hendrix, 568 So. 2d 752 (Ala. 1990)).

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Alabama project

1. **Workers' comp.** A contractor below Alabama's threshold can legally go without it, but your project requirements can still require it. Decide what you'll accept, and get an exemption or a policy in writing. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Alabama is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** Alabama doesn't set a statewide floor for a general contractor license, so your project requirements set the bar. Compare each limit on the COI with what the contract asks for.
3. **Additional insured.** The endorsement is a separate promise from the indemnity clause, so check both. Ask for the endorsement itself, not just a box ticked on the certificate.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
