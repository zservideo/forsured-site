## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 1 employee
- License insurance minimum: No statewide minimum
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: No general statute

## Workers' comp in Maine

Almost every Maine business with one or more employees must carry workers' comp. An owner of at least 20% of a corporation's voting stock can waive coverage for themselves in writing, on a form the Workers' Compensation Board approves.

Agency: [Maine Workers' Compensation Board](https://www.maine.gov/wcb/)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

We found no statewide general liability minimum tied to a general contractor license. Maine has no statewide general contractor license. Cities and counties can set their own licensing and insurance rules, so check where the project is.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Maine has no general construction anti-indemnity statute. Courts there generally enforce a clause that covers a party's own negligence only when the clause says so clearly (see Emery v. Waterhouse Co., 467 A.2d 986 (Me. 1983)).

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Maine project

1. **Workers' comp.** Nearly every contractor with employees needs it in Maine. A contractor with a crew and no workers' comp on its COI is a gap. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Maine is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** Maine doesn't set a statewide floor for a general contractor license, so your project requirements set the bar. Compare each limit on the COI with what the contract asks for.
3. **Additional insured.** The endorsement is a separate promise from the indemnity clause, so check both. Ask for the endorsement itself, not just a box ticked on the certificate.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
