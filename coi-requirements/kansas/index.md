# Kansas contractor insurance requirements

> What Kansas law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

Reviewed Oct 28, 2026 by Forsured. Source: https://forsured.ai/coi-requirements/kansas/

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: $20K annual payroll
- License insurance minimum: No statewide minimum
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: Own negligence, even in part; reaches additional insured

## Workers' comp in Kansas

Kansas employers outside agriculture must carry workers' comp once gross annual payroll passes $20,000.

Agency: [Kansas Division of Workers' Compensation](https://www.dol.ks.gov/workers-compensation/overview)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

We found no statewide general liability minimum tied to a general contractor license. Kansas has no statewide general contractor license. Cities and counties can set their own licensing and insurance rules, so check where the project is.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Kansas's anti-indemnity statute (Kan. Stat. Ann. § 16-121) voids construction contract clauses that make a contractor indemnify another party for that party's own negligence, even in part. Clauses limited to the contractor's own share of fault generally hold up. The statute also reaches insurance: a contract can't use an additional insured requirement to get coverage for the other party's negligence that the indemnity rule forbids.

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Kansas project

1. **Workers' comp.** A contractor below Kansas's threshold can legally go without it, but your project requirements can still require it. Decide what you'll accept, and get an exemption or a policy in writing. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Kansas is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** Kansas doesn't set a statewide floor for a general contractor license, so your project requirements set the bar. Compare each limit on the COI with what the contract asks for.
3. **Additional insured.** In Kansas, the anti-indemnity statute also limits what an additional insured requirement can do. Ask your broker or attorney which wording holds up, then check that the endorsement on file matches it.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
