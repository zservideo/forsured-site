# Louisiana contractor insurance requirements

> What Louisiana law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

Last checked Oct 28, 2026 by Forsured. Source: https://forsured.ai/coi-requirements/louisiana/

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 1 employee
- License insurance minimum: $100K (residential)
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: Own negligence, even in part; reaches additional insured

## Workers' comp in Louisiana

Almost every Louisiana business with one or more employees, including part-time and seasonal workers, must carry workers' comp.

Agency: [Louisiana Workforce Commission, Office of Workers' Compensation](https://www.laworks.net/FAQs/FAQ_WorkComp_EmployerCoverage.asp)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

Louisiana's licensing board requires residential and home improvement contractors to keep at least $100,000 in general liability and to show workers' comp. Commercial license applicants don't file insurance with the board.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Louisiana's anti-indemnity statute (La. Rev. Stat. § 9:2780.1) voids construction contract clauses that make a contractor indemnify another party for that party's own negligence, even in part. Clauses limited to the contractor's own share of fault generally hold up. The statute also reaches insurance: a contract can't use an additional insured requirement to get coverage for the other party's negligence that the indemnity rule forbids.

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Louisiana project

1. **Workers' comp.** Nearly every contractor with employees needs it in Louisiana. A contractor with a crew and no workers' comp on its COI is a gap. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Louisiana is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** A Louisiana license only shows the contractor met the state's floor. Compare each limit on the COI with your project requirements, which are usually higher.
3. **Additional insured.** In Louisiana, the anti-indemnity statute also limits what an additional insured requirement can do. Ask your broker or attorney which wording holds up, then check that the endorsement on file matches it.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
