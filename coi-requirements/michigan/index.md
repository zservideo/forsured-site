# Michigan contractor insurance requirements

> What Michigan law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

Reviewed Oct 28, 2026 by Forsured. Source: https://forsured.ai/coi-requirements/michigan/

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 3 employees, or 1 full-time
- License insurance minimum: No statewide minimum
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: Sole negligence

## Workers' comp in Michigan

Michigan private employers must carry workers' comp if they regularly employ three or more people at one time, or one or more people for 35 hours a week for 13 weeks or more in the past year.

Agency: [Michigan Workers' Disability Compensation Agency](https://www.michigan.gov/leo/bureaus-agencies/wdca/Insurance-Requirements/Pages/workers-disability-compensation-insurance-requirements)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

We found no statewide general liability minimum tied to a general contractor license. Michigan licenses residential builders, without a liability insurance minimum. Cities and counties can set their own licensing and insurance rules, so check where the project is.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Michigan's anti-indemnity statute (Mich. Comp. Laws § 691.991) voids construction contract clauses that make a contractor indemnify another party for that party's sole negligence. Clauses that cover shared fault are generally allowed.

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Michigan project

1. **Workers' comp.** A contractor below Michigan's threshold can legally go without it, but your project requirements can still require it. Decide what you'll accept, and get an exemption or a policy in writing. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Michigan is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** Michigan doesn't set a statewide floor for a general contractor license, so your project requirements set the bar. Compare each limit on the COI with what the contract asks for.
3. **Additional insured.** The endorsement is a separate promise from the indemnity clause, so check both. Ask for the endorsement itself, not just a box ticked on the certificate.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
