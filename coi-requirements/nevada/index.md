# Nevada contractor insurance requirements

> What Nevada law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 1 employee
- License insurance minimum: Workers' comp proof
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: Residential contracts only

## Workers' comp in Nevada

Any Nevada employer with at least one employee must carry workers' comp. The state's employer guide singles out construction trades: they must carry workers' comp even where other businesses could claim an exemption.

Agency: [Nevada Division of Industrial Relations](https://dir.nv.gov/WCS/home/)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

The Nevada State Contractors Board won't issue or renew a license without proof of workers' comp, or a sworn statement that the contractor has no employees and won't work for or bid to another contractor. A license bond is also required.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Nevada's statute (Nev. Rev. Stat. § 40.693) limits indemnity and defense clauses in residential construction contracts signed since February 24, 2015. Commercial contracts are judged under general contract law.

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Nevada project

1. **Workers' comp.** Nearly every contractor with employees needs it in Nevada. A contractor with a crew and no workers' comp on its COI is a gap. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Nevada is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** A Nevada license only shows the contractor met the state's floor. Compare each limit on the COI with your project requirements, which are usually higher.
3. **Additional insured.** The endorsement is a separate promise from the indemnity clause, so check both. Ask for the endorsement itself, not just a box ticked on the certificate.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
