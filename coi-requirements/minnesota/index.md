# Minnesota contractor insurance requirements

> What Minnesota law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

Reviewed Oct 28, 2026 by Forsured. Source: https://forsured.ai/coi-requirements/minnesota/

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 1 employee
- License insurance minimum: $100K/$300K + $25K PD (residential)
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: Own negligence, even in part

## Workers' comp in Minnesota

Minnesota has no minimum number of employees: almost every employer must carry workers' comp, even for one part-time employee.

Agency: [Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry](https://www.dli.mn.gov/business/workers-compensation/work-comp-who-needs-workers-compensation-coverage)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

Minnesota residential building contractors and remodelers must carry general liability of at least $100,000 per occurrence and $300,000 aggregate plus $25,000 in property damage, or a $300,000 single limit.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Minnesota's anti-indemnity statute (Minn. Stat. §§ 337.01 to 337.05) voids construction contract clauses that make a contractor indemnify another party for that party's own negligence, even in part. Clauses limited to the contractor's own share of fault generally hold up.

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Minnesota project

1. **Workers' comp.** Nearly every contractor with employees needs it in Minnesota. A contractor with a crew and no workers' comp on its COI is a gap. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Minnesota is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** A Minnesota license only shows the contractor met the state's floor. Compare each limit on the COI with your project requirements, which are usually higher.
3. **Additional insured.** The endorsement is a separate promise from the indemnity clause, so check both. Ask for the endorsement itself, not just a box ticked on the certificate.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
