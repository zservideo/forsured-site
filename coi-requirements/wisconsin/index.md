# Wisconsin contractor insurance requirements

> What Wisconsin law says about workers' comp, contractor license insurance and indemnity clauses, and what to check on a contractor COI for a project there.

## The short version

- Workers' comp required at: 3 employees, or $500 a quarter
- License insurance minimum: $250K (dwelling contractors)
- Anti-indemnity statute voids: Own negligence, even in part

## Workers' comp in Wisconsin

Wisconsin employers must carry workers' comp once they have three or more full-time or part-time employees, or pay $500 or more in wages in any calendar quarter.

Agency: [Wisconsin Worker's Compensation Division](https://www.dwd.wisconsin.gov/wc/employers/)

## Contractor licensing and insurance

Wisconsin dwelling contractors must show general liability of at least $250,000 per occurrence (or a bond in its place), plus workers' comp and unemployment insurance where they apply.

## Indemnity and additional insured clauses

Wisconsin's anti-indemnity statute (Wis. Stat. § 895.447) voids construction contract clauses that make a contractor indemnify another party for that party's own negligence, even in part. Clauses limited to the contractor's own share of fault generally hold up.

## What to check on a contractor COI for a Wisconsin project

1. **Workers' comp.** A contractor below Wisconsin's threshold can legally go without it, but your project requirements can still require it. Decide what you'll accept, and get an exemption or a policy in writing. For an out-of-state contractor, confirm Wisconsin is listed on the workers' comp policy.
2. **General liability.** A Wisconsin license only shows the contractor met the state's floor. Compare each limit on the COI with your project requirements, which are usually higher.
3. **Additional insured.** The endorsement is a separate promise from the indemnity clause, so check both. Ask for the endorsement itself, not just a box ticked on the certificate.
4. **Dates.** Every policy should run through the end of the job. A COI only shows coverage on the day it was issued, so check again at each renewal.
