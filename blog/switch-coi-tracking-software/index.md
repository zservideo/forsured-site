# How to switch COI tracking software without losing your history

> Changing COI tools mid-season feels risky because it is. Here's how to move without a gap in coverage checks or a hole in the record.

Published Nov 13, 2026 by Forsured. Buyer's guide. Source: https://forsured.ai/blog/switch-coi-tracking-software/

![Five steps to switch COI tracking software without losing history](https://forsured.ai/blog/switch-coi-tracking-software/hero.png)

## The short version

- Before you cancel, export every certificate, endorsement and decision, with dates and who approved it. That record is what defends you on an old claim.
- Don't migrate old pass or fail results into the new tool. Re-check every active subcontractor against each project's current requirements instead.
- Overlap the tools for one renewal cycle and tell brokers once, in writing, where certificates go from a set date.

People switch COI tracking software for ordinary reasons: the price went up, the old tool doesn't read endorsements, the company outgrew one template, or the vendor changed under them. myCOI customers, for example, are being moved to illumend, myCOI's new AI platform. Whatever the reason, the move has one rule: no week where nobody is checking, and no lost record of what was checked before.

If you're still choosing where to go, see the [full comparison of COI tracking software for construction](/blog/best-coi-tracking-software-construction/) and the [demo questions and test packet](/blog/coi-tracking-software-demo-questions/).

## Step 1: Export everything, including the decisions

Before you give notice, export:

- Every certificate and endorsement file, for active and closed projects.
- The requirements each one was checked against.
- Each result, its date, and who approved or waived it, with any note.
- Vendor and broker contacts.

Closed projects matter. A completed operations claim can arrive years after closeout, and your old file is the proof of what you required and what you accepted. Ask the old vendor in writing what format the export comes in and how long your data stays available after you cancel.

## Step 2: Rebuild requirements per project

Don't copy your old templates straight across. Use the move to fix them:

- Start from each owner's insurance exhibit and your subcontract language.
- Set one standard, then project-level exceptions where an owner asks for more.
- Check [contractor insurance requirements by state](/coi-requirements/) for anti-indemnity and workers' comp rules.

## Step 3: Re-check active subcontractors, don't import old results

An old "compliant" flag tells you what the old tool decided against old requirements. Load the current certificates and endorsements for every active trade and check them fresh. Expect the new tool to find gaps the old one didn't. That's the point of switching. Our [guide to checking a contractor COI](/blog/how-to-check-a-contractor-coi/) covers what a full check includes.

Archive closed projects as files from Step 1 rather than re-checking them.

## Step 4: Overlap for one renewal cycle

Keep the old tool read-only for at least one round of renewals. That way a certificate sent to the old address isn't lost, and you can compare results on the same trades.

## Step 5: Tell brokers once, clearly

Send one notice to every broker and trade with:

- The date certificates go to the new place.
- How to upload, and whether they need an account.
- Who to call with a question.

Repeat it in your next deficiency notice, then stop using the old address.

## Common questions

### How do I switch COI tracking software?

Export every document and decision from the old tool, rebuild your requirements per project in the new one, re-check every active subcontractor against those requirements, run both tools for one renewal cycle, and send brokers one clear notice of where certificates go from a set date.

### What should I export from my old COI tracking software?

Every certificate and endorsement file, the requirement each was checked against, the result, the date, and who approved or waived it, for active and closed projects. Closed projects matter because completed operations claims arrive years later.

### How long does it take to switch COI tracking tools?

It depends on volume and vendor. Plan for a few weeks of setup plus one renewal cycle of overlap. Ask the new vendor for their own numbers; PINS Advantage, for example, says most teams are live within a week and a full rollout typically takes 30 to 90 days.
