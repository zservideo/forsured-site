## What a service does and doesn't take off your plate

**It takes:** reading certificates and endorsements, sending deficiency notices, chasing vendors and brokers, and renewal follow-up.

**It doesn't take:** the decision to let a trade on site with a gap, the requirements you set, or the loss if the review was wrong. Your contract with the service decides how much of that they stand behind, so read it.

## Questions to ask any managed COI service

1. Who are the reviewers, and what credential do they hold? Billy names CRIS certification; ask the others.
2. Do they read endorsement wording, or check that a form number is present?
3. How do they record an accepted gap, and can you see it?
4. What happens when you disagree with a result?
5. How many attempts do they make before escalating to you? bcs says there's no cap.
6. What's the minimum? bcs publishes $10,000 a year. Ask the rest.
7. Do your subcontractors or their brokers ever pay or sign up?

## Common questions

### Can I outsource COI tracking for my construction company?

Yes. Billy, bcs, Jones and TrustLayer each offer a managed or full-service option where their reviewers check certificates and endorsements and follow up with vendors or brokers. bcs publishes full-service pricing; the others quote.

### How much does outsourced COI tracking cost?

bcs full service starts at $17.80 per vendor per year with a $10,000 annual minimum. Billy, Jones and TrustLayer quote their managed tiers. Compare it against what the same hours cost on your own team.

### Is a managed COI service better than software?

Neither is better in general. A service fits when you can't staff endorsement review. Software fits when you want your own risk team making and recording each decision. Many GCs use software and add a service only for outreach.
