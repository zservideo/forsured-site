So when a contractor's coverage lapses in the middle of your project, the certificate in your file keeps saying everything is fine.

## What does the notice of cancellation box say?

Here is the wording on the current [ACORD 25](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/12/acord-25-2025-12-liability.pdf), the 2025/12 edition:

> Should any of the above described policies be cancelled before the expiration date thereof, notice will be delivered in accordance with the policy provisions.

Older editions talked about mailing the certificate holder written notice within a stated number of days, though with a line saying that failing to do so imposed no obligation on the insurer or agent. The 2009/09 change dropped the days and handed the notice of cancellation question to the policy.

So the real question is what the policy says. Standard cancellation conditions run between the insurer and its named insured. Unless an endorsement adds you, the owner or GC on the certificate isn't on that list.

## Why can't the certificate fix it?

It's tempting to ask the agent to type a note into the ACORD 25 description box, such as "30 days' notice to the certificate holder." That doesn't create the right.

New York put this in statute. [Insurance Law section 502](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/502) says a certificate "shall not amend, extend, or alter the coverage" of the policy it refers to, and "shall further not confer to any person any rights beyond those expressly provided by the policy." It also bars anyone from willfully requiring certificate language the policy "does not expressly include." [New York's Department of Financial Services](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/insurance_companies/certificates_of_insurance) traces the rules to a new Article 5 of the Insurance Law, added by laws passed in 2014 and 2015.

The certificate summarizes. The policy decides.

## What happens if a contractor's workers' compensation or liability coverage lapses mid-project?

A lapse doesn't announce itself. The ACORD 25 contractor COI in your file still shows the old POLICY EFF and POLICY EXP dates. If the policy was canceled for nonpayment last month, nothing on that certificate changes. You find out when you check again, or when a claim comes in.

By then the cost can land on you. Workers' compensation is the clearest case, because some states, Minnesota among them, make the GC answer for a hired contractor's missing workers' compensation coverage. The [Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry](https://www.dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/infosheet_contractor_liability.pdf) explains that when a contractor a GC hires fails to carry workers' compensation, the general contractor or an intermediate contractor is liable for the benefits due that contractor's injured employees, and benefits "may be collected from any contractor up the chain." That's Minnesota Statutes section 176.215. Other states have their own rules, so check yours on the [contractor COI requirements for your state](/coi-requirements/).

[Construction Executive](https://constructionexec.com/article/construction-site-injuries-avoiding-and-limiting-liability/) makes the same point more broadly: if a contractor fails to enroll an employee in workers' compensation, "immunity can be lost," and the GC can be held liable for uncovered workers. A lapse on general liability creates a different gap. Claims from that contractor's work may have no policy to respond, and [Amwins](https://www.amwins.com/resources-and-insights/market-insights/article/when-small-contractors-face-big-claims) notes that when a contractor's insurance is insufficient, "the GC may be pulled into the claim."

## How can you hear about a cancellation in time?

Here are the ways to learn a contractor's policy ended, and what each one catches:

Table: Ways to learn a contractor's policy was canceled or lapsed
| Method | What it catches | What it misses |
|---|---|---|
| The ACORD 25 cancellation box | Nothing on its own; it points to the policy | Any notice the policy doesn't send you |
| An insurer endorsement that schedules you | Cancellation, with written notice for the days set in its schedule | Policies without the endorsement |
| A renewal certificate before each POLICY EXP date | Policies that expire without renewal | Cancellation before the expiration date |
| Ongoing verification of each contractor COI | Cancellations and expirations, for the life of the job | Nothing, if it runs on every policy your requirements list |

**Ask for a notice of cancellation endorsement.** The fix is an insurer endorsement that schedules you to receive notice. [BCS's](https://www.getbcs.com/blog/2011/08/02/how-to-obtain-a-30-day-notice-of-cancellation-despite-the-new-acord-form) example: "we agree to mail prior written notice of cancellation to the person(s) or organization(s) shown in the Schedule." Another sample BCS quotes gives at least 60 days' written notice of cancellation, or at least 10 days in the case of nonpayment of premium. BCS also warned these endorsements "are not generally available and are used sparingly by Insurers," so ask early.

**Track every policy period.** Note each policy's POLICY EXP date, including workers' compensation, from the contractor COI and ask for a renewal certificate before that date, not after.

**Keep checking for the life of the job.** A COI is a snapshot from the day it was issued. Put the check in your project requirements and repeat it, so a lapse shows up before a loss does.

## What should your project requirements say about cancellation?

Write the notice of cancellation and the follow-through into the contract, so you aren't relying on the certificate:

1. Require the contractor to keep every listed policy in force for the life of the job, and for completed operations, for the period after completion your contract sets.
2. Require a notice of cancellation endorsement scheduling you where the insurer offers one, and require the contractor to tell you directly if any policy is canceled or not renewed.
3. Require renewal certificates and endorsements before each POLICY EXP date.
4. Pass the same requirements down to every lower-tier contractor. Construction Executive calls this "an unbroken vertical chain of contractual requirements to obtain and maintain proper insurance."
5. Keep the records. Amwins recommends collecting and retaining certificates for at least 5 to 8 years.

Coverage that ends quietly is the gap a file of PDFs can't show. Check the endorsements behind the rest of the certificate the same way, starting with [completed operations](/blog/completed-operations-additional-insured/) and the [per project aggregate](/blog/per-project-aggregate-coi/).

## Common questions

### What does the notice of cancellation box on an ACORD 25 say?

Since the 2009/09 edition, it reads: "Should any of the above described policies be cancelled before the expiration date thereof, notice will be delivered in accordance with the policy provisions." The 2025/12 edition uses the same wording. It promises nothing beyond what the policy already says, and the policy usually sends notice only to the contractor.

### Can I ask the agent to add 30-day notice to the certificate?

Writing it on the certificate doesn't change the policy. New York's Insurance Law section 502 says a certificate can't amend, extend or alter coverage or give anyone rights beyond the policy, and bars requiring certificate terms the policy doesn't expressly include. The notice of cancellation has to come from the policy, usually through an insurer endorsement that schedules you.

### Is a notice of cancellation endorsement easy to get?

Not always. Certificate tracking firm BCS wrote that these endorsements "are not generally available and are used sparingly by Insurers." When they're issued, they schedule the certificate holder by name with a set number of days' notice. Ask early in the contract, and plan to monitor coverage on every contractor COI either way.

### What happens to a GC if a contractor's workers' comp lapses mid-project?

It depends on the state, and it can be expensive. Minnesota's Department of Labor and Industry says that when a hired contractor fails to carry workers' compensation, the general contractor or an intermediate contractor is liable for the benefits due its injured employees, and benefits may be collected from any contractor up the chain. Check your state's rule.

### Does a COI show that a policy has lapsed?

No. A certificate is a snapshot from the day it was issued. It shows POLICY EFF and POLICY EXP dates, but it won't change if the policy is canceled the next week. A lapse shows up only when you check again, through a renewal certificate, a notice of cancellation endorsement or ongoing verification of each contractor COI.

### How long should you keep contractor COIs?

Keep them well past the job. Amwins recommends that contractors collect and retain certificates of insurance for at least 5 to 8 years, and warns that incomplete or outdated documentation can put coverage at risk in high-severity claims involving several parties. Keep the endorsements and any notice of cancellation you receive with them.
