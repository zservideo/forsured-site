## The short version

- Vendor demos use certificates that pass. Bring five of your own that don't, and run every tool against the same project requirements.
- The questions that separate tools are about after the read: who decides, how the broker hears about the gap, what's recorded, and what happens at renewal.
- Score each demo on what you saw happen to your test packet, not on the feature list.

A COI tracking software demo is built to go well. The sample certificate is clean, the endorsement is the ISO form the tool knows best, and the broker on screen answers in a minute. None of that tells you what happens on your jobs.

This guide gives you a test packet to bring and 15 questions to ask, so every vendor is judged on the same real problems. Use it with our [full comparison of COI tracking software for construction](/blog/best-coi-tracking-software-construction/) to pick who gets a demo.

## The test packet

Pull five real certificates from your files, remove personal details, and bring the project requirements they were supposed to meet.

1. **No completed operations coverage.** An ongoing operations additional insured endorsement (CG 20 10) with no completed operations form. See [whether additional insured status covers you after the job is done](/blog/completed-operations-additional-insured/).
2. **A carrier's own endorsement form.** Not an ISO form number. The tool should judge the wording, not the number. PINS Advantage puts it this way on their site: the wording matters more than the form number.
3. **An umbrella below the owner's limit.** The certificate looks complete; the number is short.
4. **A named insured that doesn't match.** A related company, a dba, or a misspelling.
5. **A policy that renews mid-project.** So you can see what the tool does next.

## 15 questions to ask

### Reading the documents

1. Does the tool read the endorsement wording or check that a form is attached?
2. Show me the page and the language behind this result.
3. What does it do with a scanned, rotated or partial PDF?
4. The ACORD 25 says the certificate doesn't amend, extend or alter the policy. How does your tool get from the certificate to the endorsements and the policy?

### Deciding

5. Who makes the final call on each result: the AI, your reviewers or my team?
6. When my team overrules a result, where is that recorded and who can see it?
7. Can one project carry different requirements from another, with exceptions?

### Fixing the gap

8. Show me this deficient certificate going to the broker. What exactly does the broker see?
9. Does the broker or subcontractor need an account, and do they ever pay?
10. How does the corrected document come back, and is it re-checked automatically?

### After approval

11. What happens at renewal, and how early do requests go out?
12. How do you hear about a mid-term cancellation or limit change?

### Fitting in

13. Where does compliance status show up in our project and accounting systems, and can it hold a payment?
14. How long until our first project is live, and what do you need from us? PINS Advantage, for example, says most teams are live within a week and a full rollout typically takes 30 to 90 days.
15. What does the price include, and what is extra: endorsement review, integrations, users, policy review?

## Common questions

### What should I ask in a COI tracking software demo?

Ask the vendor to run your own deficient certificates against your project requirements, then ask who makes the final call, how the broker learns what's missing, where an accepted gap is recorded, how renewals are tracked, and where compliance status shows up in your project and accounting systems.

### What test certificates should I bring to a COI software demo?

Five: one missing completed operations additional insured coverage, one with a carrier's own endorsement form instead of an ISO form, one with an umbrella below the owner's limit, one with a named insured that doesn't match the subcontract, and one whose policy renews in the middle of the project. Remove personal details first.
