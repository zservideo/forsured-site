What does waiver of subrogation mean on a contractor COI?

It's the insurer's promise not to come after you once it pays a claim. A waiver of subrogation comes from an endorsement such as WC 00 03 13, not from the SUBR WVD box on the certificate, and it has to match a contract the contractor signed before the loss.

Published Nov 4, 2026Updated Nov 13, 2026

Share

The short version

  • A waiver of subrogation is the insurer's acknowledgment that it has no right to recover what it paid from a liable third party, such as the owner or GC.
  • Insurers agree to it because their insured already gave up its own right of recovery in a contract. Policies generally allow that before a loss, not after.
  • The SUBR WVD box on the ACORD 25 is a summary. The waiver comes from the policy wording or an endorsement.
  • The workers' comp form, NCCI's WC 00 03 13, applies only to parties named in its schedule, and a premium charge may apply.

A waiver of subrogation is an insurer's agreement not to go after a third party to recover what it paid on a claim. On a construction project, that third party is usually you: the owner or GC. Without the waiver, a contractor's insurer that pays for a loss could turn around and sue you to get its money back, even though the contractor promised in its contract not to.

What is subrogation?

When an insurer pays a claim, it can step into its insured's shoes and pursue whoever caused the loss. That's subrogation. In standard commercial policies, IRMI notes, the right sits in a condition called "Transfer of Rights of Recovery Against Others to Us." The workers' comp policy says it directly: "We have a right to recover our payments from anyone liable for an injury covered by this policy."

IRMI defines a waiver of subrogation as "an acknowledgment by an insurer that it has no right to subrogate against a liable third party." It gives up that recovery right against named parties, or against anyone a contract requires.

Why do construction contracts require a waiver of subrogation?

Because the contract has already asked the contractor to give up its own right of recovery against the owner and GC. IRMI explains that insurers usually agree to a waiver of subrogation as a response to the insured having waived its own right of recovery against a third party. The insurer's waiver is the proof that the contract promise holds, and that it didn't cost the contractor its coverage.

Timing matters. IRMI says policies generally don't bar coverage if an insured waives its right of recovery before a loss. But coverage is excluded from many policies if subrogation is waived after a loss, because that would violate the principle of indemnity. So the signed contract has to come first, before the contractor starts work.

Where does a waiver of subrogation come from?

Like additional insured status, a waiver of subrogation comes from policy wording, not from the certificate. Here's where it shows up, and what each piece proves:

Where a waiver of subrogation appears and what each piece proves
WhereWhat it saysWhat it proves
Your contractThe contractor waives its own right to recover from youThe contractor's promise, which must come before a loss
The policy endorsement, such as WC 00 03 13The insurer won't enforce its recovery right against scheduled partiesThe waiver itself
The SUBR WVD box on the ACORD 25A check markA summary only; certain policies may require an endorsement

The ACORD 25 says so in its IMPORTANT box: "If SUBROGATION IS WAIVED, subject to the terms and conditions of the policy, certain policies may require an endorsement." It adds that a statement on the certificate doesn't confer rights to the certificate holder in place of that endorsement. A checked SUBR WVD box on a contractor COI is where your review starts, not where it ends.

How does WC 00 03 13 work?

On workers' compensation, the standard form is NCCI's WC 00 03 13, "Waiver of Our Right to Recover From Others Endorsement." NCRB's instruction sheet describes it as used to waive the company's right of subrogation against named third parties. Its key line: "We will not enforce our right against the person or organization named in the Schedule." Three details matter:

  • It's limited to the Schedule. The form says it "shall not operate directly or indirectly to benefit anyone not named in the Schedule."
  • It can be tied to a written contract. An optional sentence limits it to work done "under a written contract that requires you to obtain this agreement from us."
  • Residual market policies get a blanket waiver only. NCRB's instructions say that for those policies, only a blanket waiver may be applied.

General liability, auto and umbrella policies each carry their own waiver of subrogation wording, often by endorsement. Ask for each one your project requirements list, and read it the same way: who it covers, and whether it depends on a written contract.

Does a waiver of subrogation cost extra?

It can. NCRB's instructions say a premium charge may apply for the endorsement on both voluntary and assigned risk policies. Tennessee's insurance department, for example, allows a carrier-set charge in the voluntary market, based on the carrier's evaluation of the exposure. For assigned risk policies, the charge is 5% of the manual premium for the work the waiver covers, with a $250 minimum per waiver. That's one reason a contractor may resist adding the waiver of subrogation until a contract requires it.

What should you ask the contractor's broker for?

When a contractor COI comes in with the SUBR WVD box checked, send one short request back to the contractor's broker. Ask for a copy of each waiver endorsement, with its form number and edition, for every policy marked SUBR WVD. For workers' comp, that's usually WC 00 03 13. Ask whether the schedule names you or uses the blanket written contract wording, and whether the waiver reaches every party your project requirements list, such as the owner and lender. If a policy has no waiver endorsement, ask the broker to add one before work starts. A waiver added after a loss may not help.

If the broker sends back a WC 00 03 13 with a blank schedule, the form names no one, and it doesn't benefit anyone not named in the Schedule. Ask the broker to fill it in.

How do you check a waiver of subrogation on a contractor COI?

  1. Confirm your project requirements call for a waiver of subrogation, and on which policies: general liability, auto, workers' comp, umbrella.
  2. Ask for the endorsement behind each checked SUBR WVD box on the contractor COI. Note the form number and edition.
  3. Check that it names you, or applies to anyone a written contract requires, and that the contract is signed before work starts.
  4. Check the related endorsements in the same pass: additional insured status, primary and noncontributory wording and completed operations coverage.
  5. Recheck at renewal, when endorsements can drop off.

Common questions

What is subrogation?

Subrogation is an insurer's right, after paying a claim, to step into its insured's shoes and pursue whoever caused the loss to get its money back. In standard commercial policies that right sits in a condition called "Transfer of Rights of Recovery Against Others to Us," IRMI notes. A waiver of subrogation gives up that right against specific parties.

Why do construction contracts require a waiver of subrogation?

Because the contract has the contractor give up its own right to recover from the owner and GC. Without the insurer's waiver, the contractor's insurer could still pay a claim and then sue you. IRMI says parties in whose favor the insured waived recovery may want proof, in the form of a waiver of subrogation from the insurer.

Does a waiver of subrogation have to be signed before a loss?

Yes, in practice. IRMI explains that policies generally don't bar coverage when an insured waives its right of recovery before a loss, but many policies exclude coverage if subrogation is waived after a loss. So the contract with the waiver should be signed before the contractor starts work, and the endorsement should be in place on the contractor COI.

Does a waiver of subrogation cost extra?

It can. NCRB's instructions for WC 00 03 13 say a premium charge may apply on both voluntary and assigned risk policies. Tennessee, for example, allows a carrier-set charge in the voluntary market and, for assigned risk policies, 5% of the related manual premium with a $250 minimum per waiver, effective July 1, 2015.

Is a checked SUBR WVD box on the certificate enough?

No. The ACORD 25 says that if subrogation is waived, subject to the policy's terms, certain policies may require an endorsement, and a statement on the certificate doesn't confer rights in place of that endorsement. Ask for the endorsement behind every checked SUBR WVD box and check that it names you or applies to anyone the contract requires.

What is a blanket waiver of subrogation?

A blanket waiver applies to any party a written contract requires, instead of listing each party by name. On workers' comp, an optional sentence in WC 00 03 13 limits the waiver to work done under a written contract that requires it. NCRB's instructions add that for residual market policies, only a blanket waiver may be applied.
