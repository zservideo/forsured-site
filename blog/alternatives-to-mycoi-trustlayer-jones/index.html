You picked myCOI, TrustLayer or Jones a few years ago, and now something has changed. Maybe myCOI became illumend and you're being moved over. Maybe the quote went up at renewal, or your team still reads every endorsement by hand. Either way, you're looking for alternatives to myCOI, TrustLayer and Jones that fit construction COI tracking: projects with different owner and lender requirements, contractors with brokers, and pay applications that shouldn't go out while a gap is open.

This guide is for general contractors' risk, compliance and project teams, and the brokers who work with them. It compares five COI tracking software tools that serve construction and answers the question most buyers start with: which one should I look at, given how we work? It also covers what myCOI, TrustLayer and Jones offer today, so you can see what you'd give up.

We compared every tool on the same six criteria, in this order of weight: endorsement review, requirements by project, how the broker gets the fix back, monitoring after approval, who makes the final call, and Procore and pricing. Endorsement review and requirements by project matter most, because that's where a certificate that looks complete still leaves you uncovered. No single tool leads on all six, so we use a "best for" format rather than a score.

Forsured is listed first. They're the alternative built around requirements set by project, with a shared workspace where the contractor's broker sees the requirement, the document and the project, and a person on your team approving each result. What do myCOI, TrustLayer and Jones offer today?

myCOI, TrustLayer and Jones still cover a lot of construction COI tracking, so know what you'd be leaving before you compare alternatives.

  • myCOI is now illumend. illumend calls itself "illumend, from myCOI," and keeps a login for returning myCOI users. Its AI, Lumie, reads every endorsement and makes each compliance decision itself. More human oversight is an add-on, and pricing isn't published.
  • TrustLayer is free up to 50 vendors on its plans page. The Procore, CMiC and Vista integrations start at the paid Pro tier, and paid prices are quoted. Its Enhanced Endorsements feature checks additional insured, primary and noncontributory and waiver of subrogation language, and is rolling out by plan.
  • Jones prices per record, per year, with unlimited COIs and no charge to your vendors or subcontractors. Its Operator tier adds a concierge team, and its Policy Review add-on reads full policies for exclusions.

If what you need is in that list, the cheapest switch may be a different tier with the vendor you have. People usually start looking at TrustLayer alternatives or Jones alternatives when the gap is endorsement depth, requirements that change by project, or price.

Which alternative fits which general contractor?

Each alternative below fits a different kind of construction COI tracking program. Every fact comes from that vendor's own pages, checked in November 2026.

Forsured: best for requirements set by project, with the broker in the fix

Forsured checks each contractor COI and endorsement against the requirements of the project it's for, including project-level exceptions. It flags gaps such as a missing additional insured endorsement for ongoing operations (CG 20 10) or an umbrella aggregate below the requirement, and explains what's wrong and what's needed. The broker sees the requirement, the document and the project in one shared workspace. Forsured keeps tracking renewals and coverage changes after approval, and a person on your team approves or waives every result.

Watch for: onboarding is by waitlist, there's no managed review service, and pricing is by quote.

PINS Advantage: best for endorsement wording checks inside Procore

PINS Advantage reads every certificate and endorsement against requirements built from your contracts, shows its reasoning and links the page it came from. It goes into the wording, such as whether a carrier's own blanket form meets a CG 20 10 and CG 20 37 requirement. Nothing is auto-approved. Plans start around $300 a month with unlimited users, most annual contracts come in at $7,500 or less, and Procore is included.

Watch for: brokers respond through an upload link rather than a shared workspace.

bcs: best for published per-vendor rates and a free tier

bcs is free up to 25 vendors with every software feature. Self-service is $11.40 per vendor per year and connects to Procore at no extra cost. Its AI identifies each endorsement type and sends every suggestion to a compliance analyst before anything is applied. Full service starts at $17.80 per vendor per year with a $10,000 annual minimum and adds full policy reviews.

Watch for: bcs isn't built only for construction, and deeper endorsement review sits in full service.

Billy: best for payment holds tied to compliance

Billy is built for construction. It syncs projects and vendors with Procore and works with accounting systems such as Sage 300, Sage Intacct, Viewpoint Vista, CMiC and JD Edwards, so a contractor with a gap is held before the check run. Its AI flags missing endorsements by form, including CG 20 10, CG 20 37, waiver of subrogation and primary and noncontributory. A managed plan uses a CRIS-certified team. Plans start at $500 a year, with a free trial.

Watch for: brokers fix gaps from a deficiency list rather than a shared workspace, and there's no full price list.

Certificial: best for live policy data from the agent

Certificial takes certificates from the vendor's agent, so it can flag a cancellation or limit change as it happens. EndorsementIQ pulls the carrier, form number and exclusions from each endorsement so you can accept or reject it against your criteria. Requestor Basic is free for up to 5 suppliers, Professional starts at $99 a month, and Enterprise is quoted.

Watch for: no Procore integration is listed, and live data depends on the vendor's agency being on the network.

Which TrustLayer alternatives and Jones alternatives work with Procore?

TrustLayer alternatives and Jones alternatives split most clearly on Procore, and TrustLayer puts its own Procore integration in the paid Pro tier. If Procore is where your project and payment teams work, these are the Jones alternatives and TrustLayer alternatives that say they connect to it, from their own pages:

  • PINS Advantage includes Procore in every plan at no extra cost. Vista costs extra.
  • bcs connects to Procore on self-service at no additional cost.
  • Billy syncs projects and vendors with Procore and holds payment through your accounting system.
  • Forsured offers a Procore integration to early access customers.
  • Certificial doesn't list a Procore integration.

Ask every vendor what status their COI tracking software writes back to Procore, and whether a gap can stop a payment. Among TrustLayer alternatives, that answer matters more than the logo on the integration page. The same goes for Jones alternatives: a connection that only syncs vendor names won't hold a pay application.

How should you pick between the alternatives?

Picking between alternatives to myCOI, TrustLayer and Jones comes down to the one gap that made you look. Test that gap first:

  1. Endorsement depth. Send one real contractor COI with a CG 20 10 and no CG 20 37. See which COI tracking software flags the missing completed operations coverage, and which passes it. Our guide to endorsement review shows how deep each tool reads.
  2. Requirements by project. Load two projects with different owner limits and check the same contractor against both.
  3. The broker. Watch a deficient COI go to a broker and come back corrected.
  4. Who decides. Decide whether you want a person on every result or a managed team. Our guide to managed COI tracking services covers the second option.
  5. The switch. Ask who imports your history into the new COI tracking software and how long it takes. See how to switch COI tracking software and the demo questions to ask.

Common questions

What are the best alternatives to myCOI, TrustLayer and Jones?

The best alternatives to myCOI, TrustLayer and Jones for construction COI tracking are Forsured for project-level requirements with the broker in the fix, PINS Advantage for endorsement wording inside Procore, bcs for published per-vendor rates and a free tier, Billy for payment holds tied to compliance, and Certificial for live policy data from the agent.

What happened to myCOI?

myCOI launched illumend, an AI platform that calls itself illumend from myCOI. Its site tells anyone who knew myCOI or came from mycoitracking.com that they're in the right place, and it keeps a login for returning myCOI users. If you're weighing alternatives to myCOI, you're weighing illumend.

Is there a free alternative to TrustLayer?

Yes, a few. TrustLayer itself is free up to 50 vendors. Among TrustLayer alternatives, bcs is free up to 25 vendors with every software feature, and Certificial's Requestor Basic plan tracks up to 5 suppliers free. Free tiers suit a small GC testing the process, not a full contractor program.

Which Jones alternatives work with Procore?

PINS Advantage, bcs and Billy all say they connect to Procore. PINS Advantage includes Procore in every plan, bcs includes it on self-service at no extra cost, and Billy syncs projects and vendors with Procore. Forsured offers a Procore integration to early access customers. Certificial doesn't list one.

Does any alternative let AI approve COIs on its own?

No, not among these five. illumend, from myCOI, is the tool that says its AI makes each compliance decision itself. PINS Advantage says nothing is auto-approved, bcs sends every AI suggestion to an analyst, and Forsured has a person on your team approve or waive every result.

How hard is it to switch COI tracking software?

Switching COI tracking software is mostly a data job: exporting contractors, current certificates, endorsements and requirements, then rebuilding requirements by project. Ask the new vendor who does the import, how long it takes, and whether your contractors and their brokers have to create accounts before they can upload.
