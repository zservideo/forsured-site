# IRMI is rewriting its design-build risk and insurance guide

> Design-build moves design responsibility onto the builder's side of the table. Project requirements, and the contractor COIs checked against them, have to keep up.

## The short version

- IRMI selected brokerage American Global to rewrite its Design-Build Risk and Insurance guide, edited by IRMI's Megan Jolly.
- The update covers progressive design-build, risk allocation, indemnification, limits of liability, professional liability, project-specific coverage and bonding.
- On design-build work, check contractor COIs for professional liability wherever a contractor carries design responsibility, not just general liability.

The International Risk Management Institute (IRMI) has picked American Global, a construction and infrastructure risk and insurance brokerage, to rewrite its long-running Design-Build Risk and Insurance guide. The team includes Caryn Maxfield, Ante Petricevic, François Wasselin and Giovanni Bach, with IRMI's Megan Jolly editing, according to the October 23 announcement.

## What the update covers

The rewrite is meant to catch up with how design-build is delivered today, including progressive design-build. It covers contractual risk allocation, indemnification, limits of liability, professional liability insurance, project-specific coverage and bonding.

## Why it matters for contractor COIs

Design-build shifts design responsibility to the builder's side of the contract. That has a direct effect on what a compliant contractor COI looks like:

- **Professional liability enters the picture.** A design-builder, and any trade contractor doing delegated design on mechanical, electrical, structural steel or curtain wall scopes, may need professional liability that a general liability policy won't provide.
- **Requirements differ by role.** The same project can need different coverage from a designer, a design-builder and a build-only trade contractor. One template for every firm misses that.
- **Project-specific programs change the checklist.** When an owner or contractor buys project-specific coverage, contractor COIs have to be checked against what the program does and doesn't cover.

WTW's 2027 outlook expects contractor's professional liability to run flat to up 5% on annual programs and flat to up 10% on project-specific policies, so the coverage is available. The job is making sure the contractors who need it actually carry it, at the limits the project requires.

IRMI's guides are a standard reference for construction risk teams, and this one is worth reading when it lands.

## Common questions

### Why does design-build change insurance requirements?

In design-build, the builder's team takes on design responsibility. That brings professional liability into the picture for the design-builder and for any trade contractor doing delegated design, so project requirements and COI reviews need to account for it.
