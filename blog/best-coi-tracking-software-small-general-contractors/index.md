Nobody's title says compliance, so the review happens when someone has time, which is usually after the trade is already on site.

## What a small GC actually needs

Skip the feature grid for a minute. For a small general contractor, four things decide whether a tool helps or becomes one more inbox:

1. **It reads the endorsements.** Expiration reminders are easy. The gaps that cost money are a missing completed operations endorsement or an umbrella below the owner's limit. See [how to check a contractor COI against your project requirements](/blog/how-to-check-a-contractor-coi/).
2. **Subcontractors can upload without an account.** If the trade's broker has to sign up for something, the COI arrives late or not at all.
3. **Someone chases the broker.** Either the tool tells the broker exactly what's missing, or a service does it for you.
4. **Setup takes days, not a project.** You need your standard requirements in, plus a way to handle the one owner who asks for more.

## The options, by how you'd start

### Start free: bcs, TrustLayer or Certificial

**bcs** is free up to 25 vendors with every software feature, which makes it the easiest way to test real COI tracking on a small job list. Past that, self-service is $11.40 per vendor per year, so 60 subcontractors runs about $684 a year at the published rate. Full service, where bcs analysts call vendors and their agents, starts at $17.80 per vendor per year with a $10,000 annual minimum, which puts it out of reach for most small GCs. bcs isn't built only for construction.

**TrustLayer** is free up to 50 vendors. Paid tiers are by quote, and the Procore, CMiC and Vista integrations start at the Pro tier. Their endorsement review shows the page behind each result and a person confirms it.

**Certificial** is free up to 5 suppliers, with Professional from $99 a month. It works differently: certificates come from the vendor's insurance agent, and Certificial flags cancellations and limit changes as they happen. That only works when the agent is on their network, and the product is aimed more at enterprise procurement than construction.

### Start cheap and construction-specific: Billy or PINS Advantage

**Billy** is built for construction only and starts at $500 a year, with a free trial. Requirements are set per project, renewal requests go out three months, one month and the day before a policy expires, and Billy also tracks lien waivers and certified payroll if you need them. A managed plan puts their reviewers on every certificate.

**PINS Advantage** starts around $300 a month with unlimited users and onboarding, and most annual contracts come in at $7,500 or less. There are no feature tiers. It reads endorsement wording against your contract requirements and your team approves each result. It's the most expensive published option here, and the deepest on wording.

### Quote only: Forsured, Jones and illumend

**Forsured** reads each COI and endorsement against the requirements of the project the subcontractor is on, and puts the broker in a shared workspace where they see the gap, the requirement and the project. A person on your team approves or waives every result. Onboarding is by waitlist, pricing is by quote, and there's no managed review service. You can test it free with a [free COI risk check](/#demo): send one project's requirements and a contractor COI or two.

**Jones** prices per vendor record per property, billed annually, and never charges vendors or subcontractors. It fits small GCs that also own property. Most broker follow-up sits in the Operator tier.

**illumend**, the AI platform from myCOI, lets AI make the compliance call and escalates only what it can't resolve. Pricing isn't published.

## The cost a small GC forgets

The subscription is the small number. The big one is time. bcs, which sells COI tracking software, [estimates manual tracking at about $36,400 a year](https://www.getbcs.com/blog/the-36000-problem-what-manual-coi-tracking-is-really-costing-you): 20 hours a week at $35 an hour. That's a vendor's estimate built on assumptions, and our COI Journal report [takes the math apart and shows how to build your own](https://coijournal.com/reports/cost-of-manual-coi-tracking/?utm_source=forsured). The point holds either way: if a free plan still leaves a project manager reading endorsements and emailing brokers, it isn't free.

Two quick ways to size it:

- **Count the COIs.** Subcontractors times active projects times policies that renew in a year. That's how many certificates someone has to read.
- **Count the chases.** How many came back wrong the first time last year? Each one is a round trip with a broker.

## How to pick in a week

1. List your next three projects and what each owner requires.
2. Pick two tools from the table: one free or low-priced, one that reads endorsements against each project.
3. Send both the same three real COIs, including one you know is short on completed operations or umbrella limits. See [which COI tracking software checks endorsements](/blog/coi-tracking-software-endorsement-review/).
4. Watch who catches the gap, how the broker hears about it, and how long the fix takes.

Check [contractor insurance requirements by state](/coi-requirements/) too. Workers' comp thresholds and anti-indemnity rules change what you can ask for.

## Common questions

### Is there free COI tracking software for small contractors?

Yes. bcs is free up to 25 vendors with every software feature, TrustLayer is free up to 50 vendors, and Certificial is free up to 5 suppliers. Billy offers a free trial. Check what each free plan reviews, because endorsement review and integrations are where plans differ.

### How much does COI tracking software cost for a small general contractor?

Published prices start at $500 a year (Billy), $11.40 per vendor per year (bcs self-service), $99 a month (Certificial Professional) and about $300 a month (PINS Advantage, where most annual contracts come in at $7,500 or less). Forsured, illumend and Jones quote on request.

### Do I need COI tracking software if I only have 30 subcontractors?

Not always. A spreadsheet can track 30 expiration dates. It can't tell you whether an endorsement covers completed operations or meets a project's requirement, and that's where small GCs get hurt. If nobody on your team reads endorsements today, software or a service that does is worth more than a reminder tool.

### Can subcontractors upload COIs without creating an account?

With most tools, yes. Billy, bcs, Jones, PINS Advantage and TrustLayer let vendors and their brokers upload through a link. Certificial takes certificates only from the vendor's insurance agent, and agents use it free.
