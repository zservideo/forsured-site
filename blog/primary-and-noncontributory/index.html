What does primary and noncontributory mean on a contractor COI?

It means the contractor's policy pays first and doesn't ask your policy to share the loss. Primary and noncontributory comes from an endorsement such as CG 20 01, not from the certificate, and that endorsement applies only when a written contract requires it and only to the general liability policy.

Published Oct 28, 2026Updated Nov 13, 2026

Share

The short version

  • Primary and noncontributory sets the order in which overlapping policies respond: the contractor's policy pays first, without seeking contribution from the additional insured's own policy.
  • Without noncontributory wording, two primary policies can end up sharing defense costs and damages.
  • It's created by an endorsement on the contractor's general liability policy, such as ISO's CG 20 01. The ACORD 25 has no field for it.
  • CG 20 01 applies only when a written contract requires it, and only to the general liability policy, not an attached umbrella.

Read the insurance section of almost any construction contract and you'll find the same phrase: additional insured coverage "on a primary and noncontributory basis." It's so common it reads like boilerplate. It isn't. Primary and noncontributory decides whose insurance pays first when a contractor's work causes a loss, and whether your own policy gets pulled in to share it.

What does primary and noncontributory mean?

IRMI defines primary and noncontributory as a term used in contract insurance requirements "to stipulate the order in which multiple policies triggered by the same loss are to respond." Split it in two:

  • Primary: the contractor's policy must pay before other applicable policies, including the additional insured's own.
  • Noncontributory: the contractor's policy pays without seeking contribution from other policies that also claim to be primary.

The County of Sonoma's insurance guide puts the primary half plainly: "The vendor's policy pays before the additional insured's policy or self-insurance are involved." The County requires additional insured coverage on a primary and noncontributory basis so that its own self-insured retention isn't touched until the limits of the contractor's policy are used up.

How does the other insurance condition split a loss?

Every general liability policy has an other insurance condition that decides how it shares a loss with other policies. Left alone, that condition can split the claim. Sonoma's guide walks through the outcomes:

How the contractor's and the additional insured's policies respond, per the County of Sonoma
Additional insured wordingHow the two policies respond
Neither primary nor noncontributory, both policies excessThey become contributing primary insurance and share defense costs and damages
Primary only, both policies primaryThey become contributing primary insurance and share the loss
Primary and noncontributoryThe contractor's policy pays first, without contribution from yours, until its limits are exhausted

So "primary" on its own isn't enough. If your policy is also primary, the two policies contribute. It takes both words, primary and noncontributory, to keep your policy out of a claim the contractor's work caused.

Which endorsement creates primary and noncontributory?

Like additional insured status, primary and noncontributory is created by policy wording, not by the certificate. The ACORD 25 has no field for primary, noncontributory or other insurance wording, and it says the certificate confers no rights on the holder. A note in the description of operations box doesn't change the policy.

What a contractor COI can do is point you to the right paper. IA Magazine advises agents to list the endorsement and its edition date on the certificate and give the holder a copy, rather than typing a general statement of coverage. Ask for the same on every contractor COI: the form number, the edition, and the endorsement itself.

In 2013 ISO introduced an optional endorsement for it, CG 20 01, "Primary and Noncontributory, Other Insurance Condition." Claims Journal explained that it was introduced in response to wording in many construction contracts, and that it alters the other insurance condition to state that additional insured coverage is provided on a primary and noncontributory basis. Whether an additional premium applies is up to the insurance carrier. Many carriers use CG 20 01 or their own version, and Jones Day recommends that owners and GCs require CG 20 01 04 13.

Primary and noncontributory rides on additional insured status, so it's only as good as the additional insured endorsement it modifies. Check both endorsements together, including completed operations coverage after the job is done.

When does CG 20 01 apply?

CG 20 01 comes with two conditions, as Claims Journal lists them. It applies only when:

  1. The additional insured is a named insured on another policy, and
  2. A written contract or agreement requires the contractor's policy to be primary and not to seek contribution from other insurance available to the additional insured.

Missing contract language means missing primary and noncontributory coverage. Write it into your project requirements, and make sure the contract is signed before work starts.

If an owner requires primary and noncontributory coverage from you, pass the same requirement down to every contractor on the job, in the same words. Public owners like the County of Sonoma require it as a matter of course. A gap between what the owner asks of you and what you ask of your contractors lands on your own policy, and the contractor COI won't show you that gap unless you compare the endorsement against both contracts.

Does primary and noncontributory cover the umbrella?

Not under CG 20 01. Claims Journal says the endorsement "applies to the CGL only, not any umbrella that may be attached." If your project requirements call for primary and noncontributory umbrella or excess coverage, the umbrella needs to say so in its own wording. Read the umbrella policy's form, or get the contractor's broker to confirm in writing which policies carry primary and noncontributory wording.

How do you check primary and noncontributory on a contractor COI?

You check primary and noncontributory on a contractor COI by reading past the certificate to the endorsements:

  1. Confirm your project requirements actually call for primary and noncontributory, and on which policies: general liability, auto, umbrella.
  2. Ask for the endorsement, not just the certificate. Note the form number and edition, such as CG 20 01 04 13.
  3. Check that it covers the parties your requirements name: owner, lender, GC and others.
  4. Check the umbrella or excess policy separately when the requirements extend to it.
  5. Confirm the written contract requiring primary and noncontributory coverage is signed and on file.
  6. Recheck at renewal. Endorsements change when policies renew, and forms keep evolving. "Evolving insurance endorsements" were the backdrop for Cullen and Dykman's session on additional insured endorsements at IRMI's 2026 Construction Risk Conference.

Two related clauses usually sit next to primary and noncontributory in the contract's insurance section: a waiver of subrogation and a per project aggregate. Check them in the same pass.

It's one line in the contract and one endorsement on the policy. Common questions

What does primary and noncontributory mean?

Primary and noncontributory means the contractor's policy must pay a covered claim against an additional insured before other applicable policies (primary), and without seeking contribution from the additional insured's own policy (noncontributory), as IRMI defines it. For a GC or owner, it keeps your own insurance out of a claim the contractor's work caused until the contractor's limits are used up.

Is primary and noncontributory the same as additional insured?

No. Additional insured status puts you on the contractor's policy. Primary and noncontributory decides the order in which that policy and your own policy pay. Construction contracts usually require both, and each comes from its own endorsement wording, so a contractor COI can show one without the other. Check for both endorsements, not just the additional insured box.

What is CG 20 01?

CG 20 01 is ISO's optional endorsement titled "Primary and Noncontributory, Other Insurance Condition," introduced in 2013. It changes the general liability policy's other insurance condition so additional insured coverage is primary and noncontributory, but only when the additional insured is a named insured on another policy and a written contract requires it. Jones Day recommends asking for CG 20 01 04 13.

Does CG 20 01 apply to the umbrella policy?

No. Claims Journal's review of the 2013 ISO changes says CG 20 01 applies to the general liability policy only, not to any umbrella attached to it. If your project requirements call for primary and noncontributory umbrella or excess coverage, check the umbrella policy's own wording, or ask the contractor's broker to confirm it in writing.

Can a COI show primary and noncontributory?

Not on its own. The ACORD 25 form has no field for primary, noncontributory or other insurance wording. A broker can type it into the description of operations box, but the certificate confers no rights and doesn't change the policy. The endorsement does. Ask for the endorsement form and edition behind every contractor COI.

What happens without noncontributory wording?

Your own policy can end up sharing the loss. The County of Sonoma's insurance guide explains that if both policies are primary, they become contributing primary insurance unless the additional insured endorsement says it's noncontributory as well as primary. Contributing policies share defense costs and damages, which is exactly what the contract's insurance section was written to avoid.
