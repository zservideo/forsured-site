A small general contractor's COI problem is time. A large one's is consistency. With 30 active projects, a dozen owners with their own insurance exhibits and hundreds of subcontractors, the question isn't whether someone can read a certificate. It's whether every project is checked against its own requirements, whether accounting knows before a check goes out, and whether you can show who approved a gap when a claim lands two years later.

This guide looks at COI tracking software from that seat. It uses the eight tools in our [full comparison of COI tracking software for construction](/blog/best-coi-tracking-software-construction/), with every fact from each vendor's own pages, linked under Sources.

## Five things that break at scale

1. **One template for every project.** Owners and lenders ask for different limits, endorsements and completed operations periods. A tool needs requirements per project, with exceptions.
2. **Status that stops at the COI tool.** If accounting can't see it, a non-compliant trade gets paid.
3. **No record of the decision.** When a gap is waived, you need who, when and why.
4. **Email as the fix channel.** At 500 trades, every deficiency becomes a thread.
5. **Review nobody can staff.** Endorsement review is reading work. Either your team does it, AI does it with checks, or a service does it.

## What to look at closely

### Requirements per project, with exceptions

Ask each vendor to load two of your real owner exhibits and show you the same trade checked against both. Watch for how exceptions work: a waived umbrella limit on one small scope shouldn't lower the bar on the next job. Our COI Journal report on [setting requirements per project and tracking renewals](https://coijournal.com/reports/per-project-requirements-and-renewals/?utm_source=forsured) covers how to structure them.

### Status in the systems that pay

[Billy](https://billyforinsurance.com/) lists the widest set of construction ERP and project integrations. [PINS Advantage](https://www.pinsadvantage.com/product/ai-coi-review) writes Approved or Not Approved to the Procore project directory and the commitment's compliance tab. [TrustLayer](https://www.trustlayer.io/plans) puts its construction integrations in paid tiers. Ask what field changes, in which system, and whether it can hold a payment.

### The record of who approved what

Every tool keeps history. The question is whether it shows the requirement, the document, the reason and the person for each accepted gap. That record is what you hand your broker, carrier or attorney after a claim. Keep the requirement version, the documents, the reason and the reviewer's name together for each one.

### Rollout

[PINS Advantage](https://www.pinsadvantage.com/pricing) says most teams are live within a week and a full rollout typically takes 30 to 90 days. Ask every vendor for the same two numbers, and what they need from you to hit them.

## Common questions

### What COI tracking software do large general contractors use?

Large GCs usually need requirements set per project, accounting and project system links, and a record of every approval. Billy, PINS Advantage, TrustLayer, Jones and Forsured all set requirements by project. Billy and TrustLayer list construction ERP integrations, and PINS Advantage writes approval status into Procore commitments.

### How do large GCs stop paying non-compliant subcontractors?

By putting compliance status where pay applications are approved. Some COI tools sync status to the accounting or project system so a payment can be held until the certificate and endorsements meet the project's requirements. Ask each vendor exactly which field it writes and which system reads it.

### Should a large GC use software or a managed COI service?

It depends on who you want reviewing. Software keeps the decision with your risk team and gives you the record. A managed service adds reviewers and outreach. Billy, bcs, Jones and TrustLayer offer both, while Forsured and PINS Advantage are software your team runs.
