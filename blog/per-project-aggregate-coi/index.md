# What does per project aggregate mean on a contractor COI?

> It means your project gets its own general aggregate limit instead of sharing one with every job the contractor works that year. A per project aggregate comes from an endorsement such as CG 25 03, and the certificate's PRO-JECT box only summarizes it. Completed operations claims still share a separate aggregate.

## The short version

- Per project aggregate means the contractor's general aggregate limit applies separately to each designated construction project, instead of one limit shared by all of its work.
- Without it, claims paid on the contractor's other jobs during the policy year reduce the same aggregate that's supposed to protect your project.
- ISO's standard form is CG 25 03. It covers ongoing operations at the scheduled projects and doesn't change the products-completed operations aggregate.
- On the ACORD 25, look for the PRO-JECT box under GEN'L AGGREGATE LIMIT APPLIES PER, then ask for the endorsement and check its schedule names your project.

Per project aggregate means the contractor's general liability aggregate applies separately to your project. Without it, one general aggregate limit covers every job the contractor works during the policy year, and claims on those other jobs use up the same limit your project is counting on.

## What is the general aggregate?

A general liability policy has two kinds of limits. The each occurrence limit caps what it pays for any one occurrence. The general aggregate caps the total for the policy year.

[IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/general-aggregate-limit) defines the general aggregate as "the maximum limit of insurance payable during any given annual policy period" for covered losses other than specified exposures. Products-completed operations claims "are paid out of a separate aggregate." Once paid losses reach the general aggregate, the limit is exhausted.

That's the catch for an owner or GC. A contractor COI showing a $2 million general aggregate tells you what the contractor bought, not how much of it is left. The [ACORD 25](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/12/acord-25-2025-12-liability.pdf) says so in its own fine print: "LIMITS SHOWN MAY HAVE BEEN REDUCED BY PAID CLAIMS." A busy contractor with a bad year on two other jobs can show up to yours with far less.

## What does CG 25 03 change?

The ISO endorsement CG 25 03, Designated Construction Project(s) General Aggregate Limit, fixes that. [IRMI](https://www.irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/designated-construction-project-general-aggregate-limit-endorsement) says it makes the general aggregate "apply separately to each designated construction project," which lets a contractor meet contract requirements for a specified amount of insurance on a specific project. [Insureon](https://www.insureon.com/small-business-insurance/general-liability/per-project-aggregate-endorsement) puts it more simply: it "gives each construction project its own aggregate limit."

The form itself, in the [CG 25 03 05 09 edition](https://ogs.ny.gov/spc-exhibit-21) published by the New York State Office of General Services, spells out how it works:

- **Each project gets its own limit.** "A separate Designated Construction Project General Aggregate Limit applies to each designated construction project," equal to the general aggregate shown in the declarations.
- **Your claims don't touch the policy aggregate.** Payments on your project reduce your project's limit, and "Such payments shall not reduce the General Aggregate Limit shown in the Declarations."
- **The each occurrence limit still applies.** The other limits continue to apply, subject to the project's aggregate.
- **A delayed project is still the same project.** A project that's abandoned, delayed or restarted is still deemed the same construction project.

Insureon works through an example. A contractor with a $2 million general aggregate has claims of $1.5 million, $400,000 and $1 million on three different projects in one policy year. Without the endorsement, those claims total $2.9 million against a $2 million general aggregate. With a per project aggregate, each claim draws on its own project's limit, and Insureon says all three would be covered. The per occurrence limit still applies to each one.

## Which aggregate does each claim reduce?

CG 25 03 sorts every claim into one of three buckets:

Table: Which aggregate a claim reduces under CG 25 03, per the CG 25 03 05 09 form
| Claim | Aggregate it reduces | Aggregates it doesn't reduce |
|---|---|---|
| Ongoing operations at a project in the schedule | That project's designated aggregate | The policy's general aggregate and other projects' limits |
| Anything not tied only to a scheduled project | The general aggregate or products-completed operations aggregate | Any designated project aggregate |
| Products-completed operations | The products-completed operations aggregate | The general aggregate and every designated project aggregate |

## Does a per project aggregate cover products-completed operations?

No, and the third row is the one GCs miss. CG 25 03 doesn't separate completed operations. IRMI confirms the endorsement "does not affect the policy's other limits, including the products-completed operations aggregate limit," and notes a separate designated project products-completed operations aggregate endorsement can make a similar change. If your project requirements call for a per project completed operations aggregate too, ask for that endorsement by name, and read [completed operations additional insured coverage](/blog/completed-operations-additional-insured/) for the rest of the after-the-job picture.

## How does a contractor COI show a per project aggregate?

The ACORD 25 has a field labeled "GEN'L AGGREGATE LIMIT APPLIES PER," with three boxes: POLICY, PRO-JECT and LOC. A checked PRO-JECT box is the certificate's summary of a per project aggregate. POLICY means one general aggregate for everything. LOC points to a per location aggregate, the related CG 25 04 form that Insureon describes for businesses with several locations.

Like every box on the certificate, PRO-JECT is a summary. The endorsement does the work, and its schedule decides which projects get their own limit. The CG 25 03 schedule is a blank list headed "Designated Construction Project(s)," with any missing details shown in the declarations. If your project isn't in that list, your claims fall into the second row of the table and share the general aggregate with everything else.

## What does a per project aggregate cost?

Usually little. Insureon says a per project aggregate endorsement "can typically be added for a nominal cost," and that there's no legal requirement to carry one. Contractors add it to meet contract terms set by clients, general contractors, lenders or bonding companies. That makes it a fair ask in your project requirements, and an easy one for a contractor's broker to say yes to.

## How do you check a per project aggregate on a contractor COI?

1. Confirm your project requirements call for a per project aggregate, and at what amount.
2. Look for the PRO-JECT box on the contractor COI, then ask for the CG 25 03 endorsement or the insurer's equivalent.
3. Check the schedule. It should name your project, or the declarations should.
4. Check the PRODUCTS - COMP/OP AGG amount on its own. CG 25 03 doesn't separate it.
5. Check the related endorsements in the same pass: [additional insured status](/blog/per-written-contract-additional-insured/), [primary and noncontributory](/blog/primary-and-noncontributory/) wording and the [waiver of subrogation](/blog/waiver-of-subrogation-coi/).
6. Recheck at renewal, when a new policy year brings a new schedule.

## Common questions

### What is a general aggregate limit?

IRMI defines the general aggregate as the maximum limit of insurance payable during any given annual policy period for covered losses other than specified exposures. Products-completed operations claims are paid out of a separate aggregate. Once paid losses reach the general aggregate, it's exhausted, and a contractor COI won't show you how much of it is left.

### What is CG 25 03?

CG 25 03 is ISO's Designated Construction Project(s) General Aggregate Limit endorsement. It gives each construction project shown in its schedule a separate general aggregate, equal to the policy's general aggregate. Claims from ongoing operations at that project reduce only that project's limit and don't reduce the policy's general aggregate or any other project's limit.

### Does CG 25 03 cover completed operations?

No. IRMI notes the endorsement doesn't affect the policy's other limits, including the products-completed operations aggregate. Under the form, products-completed operations claims reduce only that aggregate, which stays shared across all the contractor's finished work. IRMI says a separate designated project products-completed operations aggregate endorsement can make a similar change.

### Is a per project aggregate required by law?

No. Insureon says there's no legal requirement to carry a per project aggregate endorsement. It's a contract term. Many contractors need one to meet contractual terms set by clients, general contractors, lenders or bonding companies, and the contractor's insurer adds it by endorsement, typically for a nominal cost, according to Insureon.

### How does a COI show a per project aggregate?

The ACORD 25 has a field labeled GEN'L AGGREGATE LIMIT APPLIES PER, with boxes for POLICY, PRO-JECT and LOC. A checked PRO-JECT box summarizes a per project aggregate. The certificate also says limits shown may have been reduced by paid claims. Ask for the CG 25 03 endorsement or the insurer's equivalent and check its schedule.

### What is CG 25 04?

CG 25 04 is the Designated Location(s) General Aggregate Limit endorsement. Insureon explains it applies the general aggregate separately to each location instead of each project, which suits businesses with several fixed locations. On the ACORD 25, that's the LOC box. For a construction project, the form your project requirements usually call for is CG 25 03.
