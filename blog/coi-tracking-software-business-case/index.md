# The business case for COI tracking software: how to make it to your CFO

> Finance won't approve software because a certificate was late. They'll approve it when you show the hours you spend, the exposure you carry, and what each option costs against both.

Published Nov 13, 2026 by Forsured. Buyer's guide. Source: https://forsured.ai/blog/coi-tracking-software-business-case/

## The short version

- Build the case on your own numbers: certificates read per year, hours per certificate, broker round trips, and the gaps you found after a trade was already working.
- bcs, which sells COI tracking software, estimates manual tracking at $36,400 a year (20 hours a week at $35 an hour). Treat it as a vendor's example and replace it with your own count.
- Price the risk side plainly: one uninsured claim can cost more than years of software. Don't invent a number for it; show the exposure on one real project.

Most COI tracking purchases start with a scare: a trade on site with a lapsed policy, or a claim where the additional insured endorsement wasn't what the certificate implied. A scare gets attention. It doesn't get a budget line. For that, finance wants numbers they can check.

This guide shows how to build them. For the options themselves, see the [full comparison of COI tracking software for construction](/blog/best-coi-tracking-software-construction/).

## Part 1: Count the hours

Pick one recent quarter and count:

- **Certificates read.** Subcontractors times active projects times renewals and revisions.
- **Minutes per certificate,** including the endorsements. Time five of them honestly.
- **Who reads them,** and what their hour costs.

bcs, which sells COI tracking software, publishes one version of this math: [20 hours a week, 52 weeks, at $35 an hour, about $36,400 a year](https://www.getbcs.com/blog/the-36000-problem-what-manual-coi-tracking-is-really-costing-you). It's a vendor's illustration, not a benchmark. Our COI Journal report [takes the estimate apart](https://coijournal.com/reports/cost-of-manual-coi-tracking/?utm_source=forsured) and shows how to build your own.

## Part 2: Count the chases

For the same quarter, count how many certificates came back wrong the first time and how many round trips each took with the broker. Each one is time on both sides, and time a trade may be working without the coverage you required.

## Part 3: Show the exposure, without inventing it

Don't put a made-up claim figure in front of a CFO. Instead:

- Pick one live project and list every trade whose certificate or endorsements didn't meet the requirement on the day they started.
- Note what was missing: completed operations, umbrella limit, waiver of subrogation, primary and noncontributory.
- Ask your broker what that gap would mean on a claim.

## Part 4: Price the options

| Option | What it costs | What it leaves with you |
|---|---|---|
| Spreadsheet | Staff hours only | All reading, chasing and recording |
| Software your team runs | Subscription plus review hours | The decisions and the record |
| Managed service | Service fee | The decisions, and oversight of the service |

Published starting prices help anchor the range: bcs self-service at $11.40 per vendor per year, Billy from $500 a year, PINS Advantage from about $300 a month with most annual contracts at $7,500 or less. Others quote. See [managed COI tracking services](/blog/managed-coi-tracking-services-construction/) for the service side.

## Part 5: Say who carries the risk

Finance cares who is accountable. Say plainly who approves a trade with a gap today, how that's recorded, and how it changes with each option.

## A one-page template

1. **Today:** X certificates a year, Y hours, Z gaps found after start.
2. **Exposure:** on Project A, N trades started short of the requirement.
3. **Options:** three rows, with cost and what stays with us.
4. **Recommendation:** the option, the price, and the first project to run it on.
5. **Check-in:** re-count hours and gaps after one quarter.

## Common questions

### What is the ROI of COI tracking software?

It comes from two places: hours your team stops spending on reading certificates and chasing brokers, and gaps caught before a trade is on site. Count your own hours and gaps for one quarter, then compare them with each option's price.

### How much does manual COI tracking cost?

One published estimate, from bcs, a COI tracking vendor, puts it at $36,400 a year: 20 hours a week, 52 weeks, at $35 an hour. Your number depends on how many certificates you read, how long each takes, and who does it.
