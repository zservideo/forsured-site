## What are the two ways a COI compliance platform works with insurance brokers?

A COI compliance platform works with insurance brokers in one of two ways.

1. **The broker offers the platform to clients.** The agency resells or refers a tracking tool to a client, often a general contractor or property owner, who then tracks their own vendors' certificates. The broker earns revenue or a client discount and becomes the client's guide to the tool. This is what most published broker programs describe.
2. **The broker works inside the general contractor's project.** The general contractor already runs the platform. When a contractor's certificate falls short, the contractor's broker, or the general contractor's own broker, is brought into that project to fix it. This is collaboration on the job, and it's where most of a broker's certificate time goes.

The two models answer different questions. The first is "what can I sell or recommend?" The second is "how do I get this contractor cleared on this project today?" A [COI deficiency notice](/blog/coi-deficiency-notice-tools-brokers/) is the second kind of problem, and it's the one this guide puts first.

## How should insurance brokers choose a COI compliance platform?

Insurance brokers should choose a COI compliance platform by asking five questions, in this order. The CFMA's Austin chapter makes the same case for [collaboration among builders, brokers and carriers](https://austin.cfma.org/articles/bridging-the-gap-collaboration-for-compliance-success-among-residential-builders-insurance-brokers-a): compliance works when each party sees the same data.

1. **Which model is it?** A tool you resell to clients, or a workspace inside the general contractor's project? Many agencies need both, from different vendors.
2. **What does broker access show?** You want the project requirements, the exact deficiency, the document that failed and the project. A notice that only says "noncompliant" costs you a second round. Our guide to [how a GC checks a contractor COI](/blog/how-to-check-a-contractor-coi/) shows what they're reading for.
3. **Who invites the broker, and to what?** Broker access should come from the general contractor or the contractor client, and cover only the projects or contractors you serve.
4. **Who approves?** The broker fixes; the general contractor decides. Know whether a person or AI makes the call.
5. **Who pays, and what do you earn?** Get any fee, revenue share or client discount in writing, because the pages don't say.

## Common questions

### What is the best platform for insurance brokers to collaborate with general contractors on COI compliance?

The best one for collaboration on a job is a COI compliance platform where the broker works inside the general contractor's project, sees the project requirements and the deficiency together, and the general contractor approves the result. Forsured is built that way. If you want to resell or refer a tool to clients instead, TrustLayer, illumend, Billy and Certificial run broker programs.

### Do insurance brokers need their own login on a COI compliance platform?

Not always. Many platforms take a corrected certificate through an upload link with no account. A login matters when you handle many contractors on many projects for the same general contractor, because then you can see every open gap in one place instead of chasing separate emails. Ask the vendor what a broker account includes before you sign up.

### What should broker access include on a COI compliance platform?

Good broker access shows the project requirements, the specific deficiency, the document that failed and the project it's for, and lets the broker upload the fix in the same place. It should be granted by the general contractor or the contractor, and limited to the projects or contractors the broker serves, so nobody sees another agency's clients.

### Do COI compliance platforms pay insurance brokers?

Some run partner programs. TrustLayer says partners can earn revenue by reselling its software and offering review services, Certificial says agents can earn revenue through Agent Assist, and illumend offers referred clients a discount. None of the pages we read publish a fee or revenue share, so get the terms in writing.

### Who should approve a contractor's certificate when the broker is involved?

The general contractor, or the person they name. The broker's job is to fix the coverage and the certificate, not to clear their own client. TrustLayer says it doesn't approve a certificate without input from the broker and their client, and some tools let AI make the call. Ask each vendor who makes the final decision.

### Can a broker work with general contractors who use different platforms?

Yes, and most do. Each general contractor picks its own tool, so a broker with many contractor clients will meet several. Keep one internal checklist for every deficiency: get the requirement, sort coverage from paperwork, fix the policy first, then answer inside the platform the notice came from.
